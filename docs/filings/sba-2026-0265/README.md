## What the comment argues

The rule moves nearly all crop and animal production to employee-based size standards, but never names the Census of Agriculture labor measure behind the conversion. Official counts of U.S. hired farm employment differ roughly threefold depending on the measure chosen — about 2.18 million workers, 1.18 million working 150 days or more, and an annual average near 0.68 million — and hired farm employment rises more than 60 percent between January and July. The comment asks SBA to:

1. State its farm employment measure and convert it to the average-employment basis of 13 CFR 121.106.
2. Explain how it will handle seasonality now that NASS has discontinued the Agricultural Labor Survey (90 FR 42560).
3. Apply the nonemployer test used for fishing to farms, which were excluded from that comparison although 77 percent of farms hire no labor.
4. Clarify how farm labor contractor and H-2A workers are counted under 13 CFR 121.106(a).
5. Address the agencies that rely on SBA farm standards under the Regulatory Flexibility Act, keeping receipts-based farm standards until a published employee-based tabulation exists.

Every figure was checked against the Census of Agriculture 2022 tables, NASS *Farm Labor* (20 November 2024), and the rule text itself.
